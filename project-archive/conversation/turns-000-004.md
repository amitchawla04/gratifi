### Turn 1 · Claude
I like it, and R360 is one of the few companies that could actually pull it off. The part I'd change is "plug in and done."

**Why it's strong**
- You already run this for big banks. The hard parts (points, rules, fraud, paying out rewards) are 17 years solved.
- Lots of companies (retailers, apps, airlines, telcos) want a program but can't build one.
- AI agents can now do setup work that used to take a team months.

**Where it breaks**
- Plug-in loyalty software already exists (Capillary, Talon.One, Antavo and others). If you're only software, you're one of many.
- Every company's customer data is messy. That's usually where "boom, it's live" falls apart.
- A program isn't really live until rewards get delivered: vouchers, flights, cashback. That needs suppliers, money moving, and customer support, not just tech.

**The real edge**
Sell it as "your program, live in days, with R360's rewards already inside." The software gets you in the door. The rewards network is what they can't copy. It also fits straight into your non-bank push.

One question: who buys first?
1. Mid-size brands (retail, D2C, apps)
2. Big non-bank companies (airlines, telcos)
3. Agencies who'd resell it to their clients
