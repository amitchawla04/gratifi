**Gratifi, in one line:** an AI assistant that a bank builds into its card app. Customers just say what they want, and their points and card get it done.

**The problem.** Bank reward programmes are hard to use. Customers don't know what their points are worth, the catalogues are clunky, and most people end up redeeming for vouchers or not at all. Card servicing (freeze my card, why was this declined, pay my bill) sits in a separate maze of menus.

**The idea.** Replace the menus with one assistant that does everything:
- **Spend points and card on anything:** flights, hotels, rides, dining, 15-minute groceries, shopping, gift cards, subscriptions, events, experiences.
- **Manage points:** transfer to airline miles, grow them through a licensed partner, donate them.
- **Run the card:** pay the bill, freeze it, set limits, dispute charges, report fraud.
- **Look after people:** spot scams, pressure from someone else, money worries and crisis moments, and route to real help.

The customer types "two seats to Dubai next Friday, use my points". The assistant shows options as familiar app-style cards, and the customer taps once to confirm. Nothing moves without that confirmation, in the bank's own approval step.

**How the app is laid out.** Home and Explore are the shop window: banners, offers and featured products that pull people in. The real work happens in the conversation.

**Who it's for.** It's a business-to-business product. Banks put it inside their app under their own brand, as "Gratifi by R360". It runs on each bank's own rewards, rules and data, and R360's global rewards content can come on top. Each bank's version is kept fully separate.

**Why it matters for R360.** It moves R360 from running reward catalogues to owning the customer's everyday experience with their card. It also shifts redemptions toward travel, experiences and products rather than low-margin vouchers. As more brands open up their systems to AI assistants, more content can plug straight in.

**Where it stands.** There's a working prototype in 7 markets: UK, EU, India, UAE, UAE in Arabic, Singapore and Malaysia. It covers 19 categories and runs on a mock bank, with the gaps listed in the handoff doc.
