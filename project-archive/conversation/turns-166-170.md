**Three things separate a big bank from a small one:**

1. **How it's run.** Enterprise banks get Gratifi in their own cloud or on their own hardware, the premium model, a named team and their own update timing. Small banks get a managed copy from ready-made templates and go live in days to weeks.
2. **Exclusivity, limited by time and market.** A big bank buys first access, never its own version of the product:
   - new features and categories go to them first for an agreed period
   - anything we build with them stays theirs in their market for that period, then joins the product
   - R360 can sign content, such as a hotel or airline partner, exclusively for one bank in a market

   Every exclusivity has an end date and a market, so Barclays' deal in the UK never blocks a bank in Singapore.
3. **Their data, which is where the real difference comes from.** The code is the same everywhere; what each copy learns is not. Each bank's Gratifi learns only from that bank's data, inside its own walls:
   - transactions
   - account events
   - the rewards ledger
   - app behaviour
   - customer records and product holdings
   - campaign history
   - call-centre transcripts

   Over time the same product behaves differently at every bank, and the bank owns what was learned. If it leaves, that learning goes with it or is deleted. A CIO will want that promise in writing.

Small banks with little data start from R360's ready-made defaults, so they get something useful on day one. The section also covers the risks, each with its fix and owner.

1. Six months: easier to sell to other banks sooner
2. Twelve months: a stronger reason for a big bank to sign
3. Set per deal: flexible, but harder to keep track of
